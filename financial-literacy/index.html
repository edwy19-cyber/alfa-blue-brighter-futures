Financial Literacy for Life

Practical capabilities for every student

Financial Literacy for Life is an ALFA BLUE educational initiative that introduces age-appropriate financial knowledge and decision-making skills to school students. It covers money management, saving, budgeting, compound growth, investing fundamentals, risk awareness and long-term financial planning, helping young people build practical capabilities regardless of their future careers.

Why it matters

Early financial literacy builds stable lives and stronger economies

Most people make their biggest financial decisions, saving, borrowing, investing and planning for retirement, without ever having been taught how. Research shows that financial knowledge gained early pays off over a lifetime.

What the research shows

Financial education works.

A meta-analysis of 76 randomised experiments with over 160,000 participants in 33 countries found that financial education improves both financial knowledge and behaviours such as saving, with effects similar to other successful education programmes.

Kaiser, Lusardi, Menkhoff & Urban, 2022

Greater lifetime wealth.

Financial knowledge accounts for an estimated 30–40% of inequality in retirement wealth.

Lusardi, Michaud & Mitchell, 2017

Better decisions as young adults.

After personal finance education became a high-school requirement in three US states, young adults had higher credit scores and lower loan delinquency than peers in comparable states.

Brown, Collins, Schmeiser & Urban, US Federal Reserve, 2014

Smarter saving, investing and borrowing.

Financially literate adults are more likely to save, invest and diversify, to pay their credit cards in full, and less likely to become over-indebted.

Klapper & Lusardi, 2020

When young people learn to save, plan and invest wisely, they make fewer costly financial mistakes, build more secure futures and contribute to more stable, resilient economies.

From literacy to inclusion

Financial literacy today, financial inclusion tomorrow

Our mission is financial literacy: giving children the knowledge, skills and confidence to manage money well. Financial inclusion – the ability to use useful, affordable financial services such as payments, savings, credit and insurance, delivered responsibly – is the long-term outcome that literacy makes possible. Access to financial services is growing fast; the knowledge to use them well must grow with it.

Why inclusion starts with literacy

Access without understanding leaves people exposed. Research across more than 140 economies found that only around half of adults in major emerging economies who use a credit card or borrow from a financial institution are financially literate. Klapper & Lusardi, 2020

Used well, financial services change lives. In Kenya, access to mobile money is estimated to have lifted around 2% of households out of extreme poverty, with the largest gains for households headed by women. Suri & Jack, Science, 2016

Young people are already financial users. About 60% of 15-year-olds in OECD countries have a bank account or payment card, so learning to manage money safely cannot wait until adulthood. OECD PISA 2022

We teach financial literacy in the classroom today, so that tomorrow's adults can take part fully and safely in the financial system.

A state-of-the-art programme for every stage of K-12

ALFA BLUE is developing a state-of-the-art financial literacy programme that grows with each student from the early primary years to the end of secondary school. It is designed for every student, whatever career path they later choose, because everyone will earn, spend, save, borrow and plan for the future. Each stage builds on the last, using age-appropriate, hands-on and game-based learning adapted to the local country and currency.

Learning framework

A Progressive Learning Pathway

Five stages that build knowledge step by step. Age ranges are indicative and subject to pedagogical review; examples are adapted to local country and currency.

  1. Stage 1 · Ages 6–8

    Money and choices

    Understand what money is for and make thoughtful choices between alternatives.

    Principles

    Money as a medium of exchange; earning and spending; needs versus wants; scarcity and thoughtful choices.

    Practice

    Use a classroom shop or pocket-money game to choose between alternatives, set a small saving goal and explain a choice.

  2. Stage 2 · Ages 9–11

    Budgeting and saving

    Plan simple budgets and see how everyday spending affects savings goals.

    Principles

    Income, expenses, simple budgets, delayed gratification, saving for goals, safe digital payments and basic bank-account concepts.

    Practice

    Plan a monthly allowance and compare prices, identifying how small recurring purchases affect savings.

  3. Stage 3 · Ages 12–13

    Growth and risk

    Recognise how money grows over time and why higher returns carry greater uncertainty.

    Principles

    Simple versus compound interest, inflation and purchasing power, credit and borrowing costs, return versus risk, and financial scams.

    Practice

    Compare two savings scenarios using illustrative numbers and discuss why higher promised returns typically involve greater uncertainty.

  4. Stage 4 · Ages 14–16

    Investing fundamentals

    Grasp long-term investing concepts and distinguish investing from speculation.

    Principles

    Financial markets at a conceptual level; long-term investing, diversification, volatility, opportunity cost, fees and the distinction between investing and speculation.

    Practice

    Build a hypothetical diversified portfolio and explain its risks without recommending actual securities.

  5. Stage 5 · Ages 17–18

    Planning for independence

    Prepare realistic plans for income, spending, saving and long-term financial wellbeing.

    Principles

    Education and career choices, income and taxes, emergency funds, debt management, insurance basics, long-term goals and financial wellbeing.

    Practice

    Prepare a realistic first-job budget and multi-year savings plan, testing alternative income and expense scenarios.

Advanced learning experiences

At the advanced stages, students apply what they have learned to realistic, engaging challenges.

  • Stock market simulation games.

    Students manage a virtual portfolio using real market data, experiencing diversification, volatility and long-term investing without risking real money.

  • Real-world case studies.

    Students analyse real household, business and market situations to understand how financial decisions, good and bad, play out over time.

  • Competitions and challenges.

    Inter-school financial literacy and investment challenges that reward sound reasoning, teamwork and risk awareness, not just returns.

The programme is educational only. It does not provide personal investment or financial advice, or recommend any specific securities, products or providers.
